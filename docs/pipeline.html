How a voltage trace becomes a seizure count

The pipeline turns 962 hours of continuous mouse EEG into a list of seizures with start and end times, and from that into the numbers a lab actually wants: how often the animal seizes and for how long. The data changes form five times on the way.

The end-to-end flow

Each arrow is labelled with what the data is at that point, not just that something happened. Following those labels down the page is the fastest way to understand the project: a continuous voltage trace becomes a matrix, then a probability, then a list, then a statistic.

What each stage is for

One card per stage: the goal, what goes in and comes out, and the single thing that makes that stage harder than it looks. Detail diagrams land here as we build them.

1

Acquire & audit

Establish what is actually in the files. The dataset ships with a four-line description and no documentation, so everything the loader relies on had to be measured rather than read.

in  50 .mat files, 18 GB out  verified recordings + animal keys

Training files are 1000 Hz and test files 400 Hz, which nothing documents. Missed, it corrupts every later result silently — no error, just numbers that look like a modelling failure. Four test files also claim the same animal ID, so animals are keyed by filename instead.

2

Condition & window

Put every recording on the same footing — one sampling rate, one frequency band, one amplitude scale — then cut the continuous trace into the fixed-length pieces a classifier can consume.

in  continuous trace + labels out  2.0 s windows every 0.5 s

Window length is a trade. Too short and the spectrum cannot resolve the 5.9 Hz rhythm; too long and a brief seizure is diluted by surrounding baseline. 2 seconds sits inside a typical 5.6 s event while still resolving to about 0.5 Hz.

3

Featurize & classify

Reduce each window to 21 numbers that describe its shape and rhythm, then score how seizure-like it is. The score is a probability, not a yes-or-no, because every later step needs something graded.

in  window matrix out  P(seizure) per window

Ordinary theta rhythm sits at the same frequency as the seizure. Around a quarter of normal windows peak in the same 5–7 Hz band, so frequency alone is worthless — it scores below chance. The separation comes from harmonics: a sharp spike puts energy well above its own rhythm, and a smooth oscillation cannot.

Worth knowing what this is not. These numbers come from one recording, and were meant to decide what to build rather than to be a result. A feature scoring 0.91 on its own is a reason to implement it, not evidence of how the finished detector performs — that only arrives after event formation and leave-one-animal-out.

featurewhat it measures on the waveform
spectral — 8 features — how power is distributed across frequency
rel_deltaPower in 1–4 Hz. High when the trace is a slow rolling wave — deep sleep.
rel_fundamentalPower in 4–9 Hz. The seizure's repetition rate lives here. So does theta, which is why this feature alone is weak.
rel_betaPower in 9–14 Hz. Mostly the seizure's second harmonic.
rel_harmonicPower in 14–30 Hz. The energy a sharp spike deposits above its own rhythm. Theta, being smooth, has almost none.
rel_highPower in 30–100 Hz. Fast, non-rhythmic content: muscle activity, electrode noise.
harmonic_ratiorel_harmonic ÷ rel_fundamental. Literally “how spiky is this rhythm”, and the single strongest spectral feature.
spectral_entropyIs the spectrum concentrated in a few frequencies, or spread flat? Rhythmic activity is peaked; noise is flat.
peak_freqThe single loudest frequency. Nearly useless on its own — seizure and theta land in the same place.
wavelet — 6 features — the same frequency information, but localised in time
wavelet_l1 … l5Energy at five time-scales, coarse to fine. Unlike a spectrum computed over the whole window, a brief sharp event concentrates into a few coefficients instead of smearing across every frequency.
wavelet_entropyHow spread out that energy is across scales. A single scale dominating means one clean rhythm.
morphology — 3 features — the literal shape of the trace, no frequency involved
log_line_lengthTotal up-and-down distance travelled. Imagine straightening the squiggle into a line and measuring it: spiky signals travel much further than smooth ones of the same height.
log_varianceAmplitude. How far from flat the trace sits.
kurtosisHow heavy the tails of the amplitude distribution are. Occasional big spikes against a quiet background produce heavy tails; a steady oscillation does not.
rhythmicity — 2 features — how regularly the shape repeats
autocorr_peakSlide the window against a delayed copy of itself: how well does it match? A metronomic spike train matches strongly, irregular activity of the same power does not.
autocorr_lag_hzThe delay at which that best match occurs, expressed as a rate. This is the repetition frequency measured in the time domain rather than the frequency domain.

The artifact group was removed. Two amplitude heuristics (clipping fraction, amplitude range) were built to stand in for the EMG-based artifact rejection the specification assumes. They measured as dead weight — removing them slightly improved the result — so the shipped set is 19 features, not 21.

4

Form events

Turn a stream of per-window scores into discrete seizures with a start and an end. Overlapping windows are averaged into one trace per moment, thresholded with a hysteresis rule, then grouped — brief dropouts merged, fragments discarded.

in  P(seizure) per window out  (onset, offset) list

This stage decides whether a seizure is counted once, three times, or not at all. It is where the result is won: with the classifier held fixed, moving one threshold took event F1 from 0.50 to 0.80 and cut false alarms from 44.6/h to 3.5/h.

5

Evaluate & report

Match detected events against the annotator's, and turn the surviving list into seizure burden — the number a lab actually uses. Scoring is done with the community reference implementation so results compare to published work.

in  detected + annotated events out  per-animal metrics + burden

The scoring library's defaults are built for human seizures — a 30-second tolerance around events that here last five. Left alone they inflate every number without raising an error, so all five parameters are overridden at rodent scale in one place that nothing can bypass.

How it was trained and validated

This is not a pipeline stage — no data is transformed here. It is the protocol wrapped around stages 3 to 5, and it is what makes the numbers mean anything. Two things are easy to conflate: cross-validation is the measuring procedure; an ablation is an experiment that repeats that whole procedure with something taken away. The first figure is the outer program — every experiment that ran, and what each one decided. The second is what happens inside any single one of them.

Three things people expect to be here, and are not

No hyperparameter search. CatBoost ran at 400 iterations, depth 6, learning rate 0.1 for every experiment in the project. That was deliberate: the Phase 1 result showed window-level quality barely moved while the event-level result nearly doubled, so tuning the classifier would have been effort spent on the part that was already good enough.

No joint training. The gradient-boosted model and the U-Net are two separate experiments. The U-Net replaces stages 2 and 3 entirely — it reads raw signal and emits one probability per sample, so it has no windows and no features — and then runs through identical stage 4 and 5 code. That is what makes the comparison about architecture rather than about scoring.

Three levels, and the middle one is easy to miss. The 18 animals split into 8 used for development and 10 reserved. Inside the 8, each fold holds out two animals — one to set the threshold, one to score — and fits on the remaining six, producing eight separately trained models. The reserved 10 appear in none of that: not in training, not in validation, not in any fold’s score. They are evaluated once at the end by a single model refit on all 8 development animals, with no fold structure at all.

No tuning against the test split. The ten held-out mice were evaluated exactly once, with both models trained on all eight training animals and thresholds fixed beforehand at the median of the eight folds. A held-out number you adjust and re-check is no longer held out.

Build order

The pipeline was built thin end-to-end first — a deliberately weak model carried all the way through to event-level scoring — so that every later change had an honest number to be measured against from day one.
